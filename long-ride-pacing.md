# Long Ride Pacing Guide

Reference for all long bike sessions (3h+) in Cycle 7 and taper, indoor and outdoor.

## Core principle

**HR is the cap. Watts are a check, not a target.**

FTP is a physiological capacity that does not change between trainer and outdoor. What changes is the *expression* of that capacity (wind, coasting, position, heat, mental load). Race-pace HR is what your aerobic system can sustain for 6 hours. Whatever watts that produces on a given day is your number.

If watts are higher than expected at target HR, you are fitter than the model. If watts are lower at target HR, that is heat or fatigue, investigate. Either way, HR drives the decision.

---

## HR targets for the 6:30 race-pace long ride

Revised May 18, 2026 for 6:30 race target (was tuned for 6:00 stretch).

| Block | HR target | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 - 0:30 (warmup) | 118-128 | Real warmup, not race pace. Do not glance at watts. |
| 0:30 - 1:30 | 128-133 | Settle in. **Hard cap 133 in hour 1, no exceptions.** |
| 1:30 - 4:00 | 132-138 | Race-pace endurance, the bulk of the ride. |
| 4:00 - 6:30 | 136-142 | Race-pace peak. Allow drift here, not earlier. **Watch the gut, not just the HR.** |
| T2 transition + run | <140 first 5min | Settle off the bike before lifting effort. |
| Run first 30min | 130-145 | Start 130-135, drift to 142-145. Cap 148. |

**Target average over 6:30: ~135-137 bpm.**

For shorter rides (3-4h), cap drift earlier: aim for avg HR 130-134.

## First hour discipline

Cardiac drift at endurance intensity adds 5-8 bpm over 4-6h at the same external effort. Heat, dehydration, glycogen, central fatigue, all unavoidable. Plan accordingly.

If you start at 142 in hour 1, you finish at 148-150 by hour 4. Above target.
If you start at 132 in hour 1, you finish at 138-142. Race pace held to the line.

**You can only save HR room at the start. You cannot claw it back later.**

### Rules for hour 1

1. **HR over 135 → soft-pedal.** Drop watts by 20-30W until HR comes down. Do not try to ride through it.
2. **No looking at watts for the first 30min.** Watts will trick you into pushing. HR is the only metric.
3. **Do not skip the warmup.** 30 minutes at 120-128 sounds like wasted time but it is the difference between sustainable and cooked.
4. **If you are at 135 with no warmup done**, you are already in trouble. Stop, drink, refocus, restart.

### Sanity check at hour 1

When the clock hits 1:00, look at three numbers:

- **Avg HR for the hour:** should be ≤132. If 135+, you are over.
- **Current HR:** should be 130-135.
- **RPE:** should feel 4/10. If already 6/10, you are cooked.

If two of three are off, dial down now. Hour 2 will not fix hour 1.

## Why riders start hot

Common causes and how to fix:

- **Caffeine pre-ride.** Coffee adds 5-8 bpm baseline. If using caffeine, take it 30min into the ride, not before.
- **Stress or unsettled morning HR.** Check resting HR before starting. If it is 8+ above your normal baseline, your starting line is shifted up; soften the first hour even more.
- **Not actually warmed up.** Most riders think they did a warmup but rolled at 200W from minute 5. A real warmup is HR-defined: 15min until HR is "fired up but easy" (~120), then 10-15min ramping to 130, *then* start.
- **Mental gun-it instinct.** Fresh legs feel great, watts come easy, HR follows. By the time you notice, you are 3 bpm over and committed.

## Indoor vs outdoor: are the targets the same?

**Yes for HR. No for watts.**

The HR table above applies identically indoor and outdoor. Your aerobic system, lactate threshold, and heart do not know which room you are in.

Watts will differ, sometimes meaningfully:

### Indoor (trainer)

- **Hotter.** No wind cooling, ambient air heats up fast. Expect HR to run 3-5 bpm higher at the same wattage compared to outdoor in cool conditions. Use a fan; dial back watts if HR drifts.
- **No coasting.** Power is constant for the entire session, no descents, no traffic. This makes the trainer harder than outdoor for the same average watts. NP and avg power are nearly identical indoor.
- **Position is rigid.** No bumps, no road changes. Pelvic and shoulder fatigue arrive earlier than outdoor.
- **Mental load is different.** Boredom and willpower are the limiters as much as the legs.

Practical implication: at HR 138, indoor watts might be 210-220W. Trust the HR.

### Outdoor

- **Wind cooling lowers HR.** Same effort, HR runs 2-5 bpm lower than indoor. You will feel like you can do "more" at the same HR. That is real, but the watts gain is from cooling, not fitness.
- **Coasting and braking.** Descents, traffic, signals create unintended micro-recoveries that fragment the effort. NP can be 5-10% higher than avg power.
- **Wind gusts force micro-adjustments.** Wattage drops you do not see on the trainer.
- **Sustained climbs and headwinds.** Will push HR above target. Hold the HR cap; let watts go where they go.

Practical implication: at HR 138, outdoor watts might be 195-215W (5-10% lower than indoor at same HR). That is normal, not a deficit.

### Same HR, different watts: a worked example

| Scenario | HR | Likely watts |
|---|---|---|
| Indoor trainer, fan on, cool room | 138 | 215-225W |
| Indoor trainer, hot room, no fan | 138 | 200-215W (heat tax) |
| Outdoor, cool day, no wind | 138 | 215-225W |
| Outdoor, headwind | 138 | 195-210W (real wattage) |
| Outdoor, descent or tailwind | 138 | 230W+ briefly |

Same HR, different external conditions, different watts. None of them mean you are fitter or less fit. They mean conditions are different.

## Race-day application

Hamburg June 7, 2026, the bike leg is 180km targeting **6:30 (27.7 km/h sustained)**. Revised May 18 from 6:00-6:15 stretch to 6:30 realistic, see `race-honest-take.md`.

- **Race-pace HR target: 135-140 sustained**, drift to 142 acceptable in final hour
- **Race-day watts at FTP 255W (Wk26 test):** 70-78% FTP = **180-200W avg outdoor**, NP 195-210W
- Indoor watts at same HR will read 10-15W higher (heat tax, no coasting)
- Going out at 220W+ on race day is how you blow up at km 120 and walk the marathon
- **Gut tolerance is something to pay attention to, not a major risk.** Cycle 6 220km / 7h05 ride managed nutrition fine. Wk27 indoor symptoms at 4-5h with all-gel fueling treated as outlier (heat + monotony + cast). Mixed fuel (gels + chews/bar + cola) Wk29 Sat keeps tolerance open.
- **Cadence is the early warning.** If cruise cadence drops from 75-80 to 70 or below, you're cooking the legs. Back off 5-10W and refuel.
- The trainer rehearsal at HR 135-140 is the rehearsal that matters, not the watts.

Frankfurt 2024 was 7:17 on the bike at higher avg HR. Hamburg 2026 6:30 happens at *similar or lower* avg HR than Frankfurt, on a lighter body with more watts. Aerobic efficiency + position + pacing discipline, not HR ceiling, is the lever now.

### Race-day HR reality check vs Wk27 race-sim

Wk27 indoor 6h30: NP 159W / HR 133 avg / hit gut wall at 5h. That was *below* the race-target HR band but the gut still went. Outdoor race-day will have:
- Better cooling (5 bpm lower HR for same effort)
- Position changes (relief on the gut)
- Real distractions (mental load shifts attention from gut)

These help. But not enough to assume the wall moves from 5h to 7h. **Treat the 4h mark as the inflection: by then, all fueling should be locked in and the gut should still be accepting. If not, switch immediately to fluid+sodium only and hold the bike split, don't try to recover by eating more.**

## Quick rules summary (revised May 18 for 6:30 target)

1. HR is the cap. Watts are a check.
2. Hour 1 hard cap: **133 bpm**. No exceptions.
3. Warmup is mandatory and HR-defined, not time-defined.
4. Indoor vs outdoor: same HR targets, different watts is normal.
5. If two of (avg HR, current HR, RPE) are off at hour 1, dial down immediately.
6. **Race day: 180-200W avg outdoor, HR 135-140, drift to 142 in final hour.**
7. **Gut variety beats gut ceiling.** All-gel hit symptoms at 4h in Wk27 indoor. Mix gels + chews + bar + cola to keep tolerance open. Reduce intake before switching to fluid only.
8. **Cadence is the bonk tell.** Cruise 75-80. If it drops to 70 or below, back off 5-10W.
9. The goal of a long ride is rehearsal, not a power record.
