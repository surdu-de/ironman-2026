## Taper Principles

1. **Volume comes down. Intensity stays.** Standard Ironman taper. Drop weekly hours by ~45% Wk29, ~70% Wk30. Keep race-pace and short sharp efforts.
2. **Address Garmin Anaerobic Shortage.** Add short strides + short VO2 openers. Sharpens the engine and matches race-day anaerobic moments (swim start, transitions, late marathon).
3. **No new stimulus.** No new shoes, no new gels, no new wetsuit, no new bike fit.
4. **Sleep is the #1 priority.** Recent sleep flagged as "Poor" in Garmin Readiness. Protect 8h every night Wk29, 8h+ every night Wk30.
5. **Carb load Thu-Fri-Sat of race week.** Not before. Glycogen window is 36-48h.
6. **Mental rehearsal.** Visualize race scenarios in Wk29-30, especially the 4-5h gut wall point and how to respond.

## Race-Day Strategy (Jun 7, 2026)

Revised May 18 from Apr 29 placeholder targets.

### Swim (3.8km) — Target 1:55

- **Conservative start.** Don't sprint the first 200m. Find feet by 300m.
- **Steady rhythm** through both laps. Sight every 6-8 strokes.
- **Draft when possible** (legal in swim). Save 5-8% energy.
- **No panic on contact** in the first 500m. Roll with it, breathe out.
- **Pace math:** 1:55 finish over 3.8km = **3:02/100m average**. 1:50 = 2:54/100m. Frankfurt 2024 was 1:57 = 3:05/100m.
- Wk25 indicators: 750m @ 1:59/100m sustainable, 300m @ 2:06 comfortable. You don't need to hold 1:59 for 3.8km, holding 3:00/100m with drafting is the actual race-day ask.

### T1 — Target 4-5 min

- Helmet, glasses, sunscreen, bike shoes. **Practice in Wk30 brick.**
- Cast-affected wrist mobility: pre-position helmet straps loose, easier to don.

### Bike (180km) — Target 6:30

**Pacing plan by hour (see `long-ride-pacing.md` for full HR-by-block):**

| Hour | HR target | Watts (outdoor avg) | Cadence | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | 128-133 (hard cap 133) | 175-185W | 80-85 | Settle. No watts glance for 30min. |
| 1-2 | 130-135 | 180-190W | 80-85 | Find rhythm. Lock in fueling. |
| 2-4 | 132-138 | 180-195W | 78-83 | Race-pace endurance. Cruise. |
| 4-5 | 134-138 | 180-195W | 78-83 | **Gut check.** Variety in fuel should keep tolerance open. If symptoms appear, swap to chews + cola, don't push more gels. |
| 5-6:30 | 136-142 | 180-200W | 75-80 | Allow drift. Watch cadence — if drops to 70, back off 5-10W. |

**Nutrition (mixed fuel strategy, refined Wk29 Sat):**
- ~80g carbs/h from minute 15 (first gel within 10-15min of mount)
- **Variety:** gels first 2h → chews/bar block 2-4h → gels + cola + **course bananas** from 4h
- Course bananas (real food, low osmolarity, easy on the stomach) are a free reset for fuel fatigue, grab them
- 600-800ml fluid/h, 500-1000mg sodium/h
- **If gut struggles:** drop to 40-50g/h + chews + cola + bananas, hold pace, don't push more gels

**Tactical:**
- Stay legal-aero. Don't draft, don't get a penalty.
- TT position default. Sit up at aid stations + every 30min for back relief.
- **Don't decide to push at km 60.** Decide at km 140 if you stole something.

### T2 — Target 3-4 min

- Run shoes only. Visor + race belt. Move.

### Run (42.2km) — Target 4:30

**Pacing by 10km block:**

| Block | Target pace | HR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| km 0-10 | 6:30-6:40/km | <140 | **Hold back.** Legs feel fresh but you have 32km to go. |
| km 10-21 | 6:20-6:30/km | 140-148 | Settle into MP. Aid stations: water + cola from km 15. |
| km 21-32 | 6:20-6:35/km | 145-152 | The grind. **HR 148 cap is gone here, but RPE must stay sustainable.** |
| km 32-42 | by feel | up to 158 | Final 10km. If you have it, push from km 35. If not, run-walk through aid stations and hold pace between. |

**Nutrition:**
- Gel every 4km from km 4 (10 gels total)
- Cola from km 15 (every aid station)
- Water + electrolyte at every aid station

**The "if it goes wrong" plan:**
- **Cramps?** Sodium + cola + walk 1 aid station, then resume.
- **Stomach off?** Cola + water only for 2 aid stations, no gels.
- **Legs gone?** Run-walk strategy: 8min run / 1min walk through aid stations. Total time loss ~10min vs continuous. Better than blowing up.

## Race Result (Jun 7, 2026), DNF at 120km

**Swim 1:51:19, then a deliberate stop on the bike at km 120. The day was ended by course conditions and a sabotage event, not by the body or the plan.**

**Conditions:** rain most of the day, plus wind. Cold, wet, hard on the bike. Hamburg road surface was poor on top of that.

### Swim (3.8km), 1:51:19, target beaten

- **3,921m / 1:51:19 / avg 2:50/100m / HR 130 avg, 153 max / avg distance per stroke 1.28.**
- 8 splits of ~500m (pace per 100m): 2:23, 2:23, 2:44, 2:58, 3:32, 3:07, 2:49, 2:46. HR per split 115-137.
- First 500m felt long and a bit anxious. Settled into the second lap by keeping heart rate and breathing steady.
- **Still beat the 1:55 target** (held 2:50/100m, faster than Frankfurt 2024's 1:57). With the new plan I'd hoped for a faster swim, but a month without proper swim training (cast) cost a couple of minutes. Given that, this was a good swim.

### Bike (ended at 120km), DNF

- **Avg speed 22-24 km/h, HR 132 avg over the leg, ~4:55 elapsed at the 120km point.**
- **High HR early.** First half hour HR was over 160 despite almost no effort, kept the effort low on purpose and didn't push it higher. HR would not come down, a mix of race excitement, cold, and rain rather than work rate.
- **TT bars came loose around km 10.** The aero bars were almost falling off. First mechanical problem of the day.
- **Course sabotage.** Someone scattered metal shards on the bike course. Hundreds of athletes got flats, some were injured. I flatted too. With help from people around I fixed the tire, but I'd lost a lot of time and the day was no longer going to plan.
- **The km 120 decision.** Wrist still not fully recovered and the pressure building. Rather than ride a second lap back through the shard zone on tubed tires, I chose to stop at km 120 to avoid another flat or an injury. Athletes on tubeless setups could ride through and finish, tubed tires could not, and the Ironman crew had run out of spare tubes. A hard call, but the right one.

### Run (42.2km)

- **Did not start.** Day ended on the bike.

### Honest read

The swim beat target and the bike was being ridden on plan (HR 132, in the 130-138 band) when the day came apart for reasons outside my control: weather, a loose TT bar, and a sabotaged course that flatted hundreds of riders with no spare tubes left. Stopping at km 120 was a deliberate safety decision, not a blow-up. The gut-wall and pacing risks the whole plan worried about never got tested. The fitness arrived. I'm at peace with the calls I made on the day.

**Forward:** switch to a tubeless setup, and don't give up on future competitions. Some things weren't in my control here, and I'm happy with how I handled the ones that were.
